# Incident: Certificate Expiry Disables All Firefox Add-Ons
Date: 2019-05-04
Service: firefox-addons
Severity: P0
Root Cause Tag: certificate-expiry

## Summary
Most Firefox add-ons (~15,000) stopped working when an intermediate signing certificate expired. Firefox requires a valid certificate chain to verify add-on integrity and prevent malware. The expired certificate caused all add-ons to fail verification and be disabled, affecting hundreds of millions of Firefox users for 15-21 hours.

## Timeline
- May 3 23:00 UTC — Intermediate certificate expires
- May 4 01:00 UTC — Reports of add-ons being disabled begin appearing
- May 4 06:00 UTC — Mozilla confirms certificate expiry as root cause
- May 4 09:00 UTC — Mozilla pushes Studies hotfix to inject valid certificate
- May 4 14:00 UTC — Hotfix reaching majority of users via Studies system
- May 4 18:00 UTC — Follow-up Firefox update released for remaining users

## Root Cause
Mozilla's add-on signing infrastructure uses a certificate chain to verify that add-ons have been reviewed and approved. An intermediate certificate in this chain had a fixed expiration date that was not monitored by any automated alerting system. When it expired, Firefox's runtime signature verification failed for every installed add-on, causing the browser to disable them all as a security precaution. The certificate rotation process was manual and had no calendar reminders or automated renewal.

## Impact
- ~15,000 add-ons disabled across hundreds of millions of Firefox installations
- Ad blockers, password managers, privacy tools, and productivity extensions all stopped working
- 15-21 hours before the fix reached most users
- Some users lost add-on data and customizations during the re-enable process

## Mitigation
- Immediate: pushed a privileged "Studies" add-on that injected a valid certificate into Firefox's cert store
- Short-term: released Firefox updates with the renewed certificate
- Long-term: implemented automated certificate expiry monitoring and alerting; reduced certificate lifetimes with automated renewal

## Lessons Learned
1. Certificate expiry is a predictable failure — automate monitoring and alerting for all certificates.
2. Hard-coded certificate expiration dates without monitoring are ticking time bombs.
3. The blast radius of a signing certificate is every artifact it signs — potentially the entire add-on ecosystem.
4. Recovery mechanisms (like Firefox's Studies system) must exist for pushing emergency fixes.
